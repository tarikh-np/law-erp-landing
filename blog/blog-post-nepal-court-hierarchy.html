Nepal Court System

Nepal Court Hierarchy Explained:
Supreme Court, High Courts & District Courts

A practical guide for Nepal law firms — understanding the court system from Supreme Court down to District Courts, and why it matters for case management.

By Tarikh Team · September 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Nepal's court system is organized into a clear hierarchy that every law firm must understand. Whether you're handling a case in a District Court in Kathmandu or an appeal before the Supreme Court, knowing which court has jurisdiction and how cases move through the system is essential for effective case management.

The Three-Tier Court Structure

Nepal's judiciary operates on a three-tier system: the Supreme Court at the apex, High Courts at the provincial level, and District Courts at the local level. Each court has distinct jurisdiction and handles different types of cases.

1. Supreme Court (अन्तिम अदालत)

The Supreme Court is the highest court in Nepal, located in Kathmandu. It serves as the final court of appeal and has original jurisdiction in certain matters. The Supreme Court hears appeals from High Courts, issues writs, and interprets the Constitution. For law firms, Supreme Court cases are typically high-stakes constitutional matters, major appeals, and precedent-setting decisions.

2. High Courts (प्रादेशिक अदालतहरू)

Each of Nepal's seven provinces has a High Court. These courts hear appeals from District Courts and have original jurisdiction in certain cases. High Courts handle more complex civil and criminal matters, and they play a critical role in the appellate process before cases potentially reach the Supreme Court.

3. District Courts (जिल्ला अदालतहरू)

District Courts are the court of first instance for most cases in Nepal. There are 77 District Courts — one for each district. These courts handle the vast majority of civil and criminal cases, including property disputes, family law matters, criminal cases, and contract disputes. For most law firms, District Courts are where the day-to-day work happens.

Specialized Courts and Tribunals

Beyond the three-tier system, Nepal has specialized courts for specific types of cases. These include the Court of Appeal (for certain specialized matters), Labor Courts, Revenue Courts, and various tribunals. Understanding which specialized court handles which type of case is important for proper case classification.

Why Court Hierarchy Matters for Law Firm Case Management

For Nepal law firms, tracking which court is handling each case matters for several reasons. Different courts have different procedures, different filing requirements, different hearing schedules, and different Peshi/Tarikh date conventions. Case management software that understands Nepal's court hierarchy — like Tarikh — can automatically route cases to the right court templates, set appropriate reminders, and track proceedings correctly.

When you manage matters across multiple courts — a District Court case here, a High Court appeal there — you need a system that understands the differences. The hearing types, case stages, and documentation requirements vary by court level. A generic legal software from another country simply doesn't have this built in.
