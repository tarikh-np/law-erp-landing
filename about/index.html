About Tarikh

Tarikh is law ERP and legal practice management software for Nepal

Tarikh brings matters, court hearings and Peshi dates, clients, documents and NPR billing into one system for law firms in Nepal.

What Tarikh is

Tarikh is legal practice management software — often called a law ERP — built for law firms in Nepal. A firm uses it to open and track matters, record court hearings and Peshi or Tarikh dates, keep client and party records, store documents against the right matter, and raise invoices in Nepali Rupees (NPR).

Schedules can be worked in the Bikram Samvat calendar alongside AD dates, and case data is structured around the way Nepal's courts are organised, from District Courts through to the Supreme Court.

The name

Tarikh (तारिख) is the date a court sets for a case hearing. It is the date a Nepali law firm plans its week around, which is why the product is organised around Tarikh and Peshi (पेशी) dates rather than around a generic task list.

Who builds it

Tarikh is built and maintained by the team at Comply Nepal, a software company working with legal and compliance teams in Nepal. Tarikh is the product the team builds for law firms specifically; the parent organisation is Comply Nepal.

The team is based in Kathmandu, Nepal, and works with firms across the country.

Who it is for

Tarikh is for solo advocates, small firms and growing practices in Nepal that want their matters, court dates, documents and billing in one place instead of spread across diaries, spreadsheets and chat threads.

If your firm handles litigation, family, property, corporate or advisory work in Nepali courts, the structure adapts to how you already work.

What Tarikh is not

Tarikh is not legal advice and it does not practise law. It is software that records and organises the information a firm already manages, so that dates, documents and billing stay visible to the people responsible for them.
